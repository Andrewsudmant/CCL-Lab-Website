# ADR 0006: Site-level reader value and lighter public scaffolding

- Status: accepted for Gate 5F owner review
- Date: 2026-08-28

## Context

Gate 5E established a sound structured reader-value model, but the public site still asked readers to assemble its strongest claim and exposed many internal editorial labels. Theme cards repeated definitions, Work pages opened as database records, and 24 equally weighted idea cards lacked a reading hierarchy.

## Decision

The homepage states one principal claim and adds three routes for researchers, policy and practice, and prospective students or collaborators. Its four theme cards use approved propositions that destabilise familiar assumptions instead of repeating definitions.

Theme and idea records retain their full structured editorial fields. Public theme pages expose one proposition, one restrained boundary and a later cycle connection. Idea cards combine the problem and consequence, show at most three separately governed public method tags, and distinguish two signature questions from four additional directions. This is narrative hierarchy, not a pipeline, ranking, priority or funding signal.

Work pages lead with a problem, central question, approach and bounded reader value before evidence status, outputs and metadata. This supports heterogeneous programmes, papers, projects and tools without inventing a parent structure or unsupported findings.

Our Approach uses six points where the four-theme cycle can break down. Its active-travel example is explicitly hypothetical so it clarifies the relationship among questions without claiming evidence, advice, scoring or transferability. Current Conversations begins with the reader problem it may later address while remaining disabled and in development.

Previous-work selection, order, theme relationships and public rationales remain frozen. A private proposal records possible later reader-facing revisions, but Gate 5F does not publish them.

## Consequences

Pages should feel less templated while the data remain more auditable than the visible interface. Tests must protect both layers. Later changes to signature status, public method tags, Work claims or previous-work copy require documented review. No API, deployment or publication authority follows from this ADR.
