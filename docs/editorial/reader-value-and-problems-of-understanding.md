# Reader value and problems of understanding

Public research copy must show what a reader can understand differently after reading it. A list of topics, locations, populations or methods does not by itself establish research value.

## Theme argument

Each theme should move from a stable but incomplete understanding, through an instability or contradiction, to a consequence for research or decisions. It should then state the theme's analytical intervention, the changed understanding that follows, and an explicit boundary on what the theme does not claim.

In compact form:

`stable but incomplete understanding → instability → consequence → intervention → changed understanding → boundary`

The public `What this changes` sentence makes the conceptual payoff visible. It is not a promotional impact claim.

## Avoid the gap trap

An absent study, missing place, unrepresented population or unavailable dataset is not automatically a valuable research priority. A gap becomes consequential when it prevents a community from resolving an important question, destabilises an inference, conceals an exclusion that changes the answer, prevents a decision, or limits what cities can learn.

This distinction is central to *Where New Evidence Matters*: the size of an evidence gap and the value of filling it are different questions.

## Research ideas

Every public idea needs a named problem or instability, a question capable of improving understanding, a consequence explaining why the answer may matter, and a possible research design explaining how evidence could distinguish among explanations. Methods must be connected to that design; they are not a decorative inventory.

Ideas remain possible directions. Their exact disclaimer is `Research idea · not currently an active or funded project`. An idea must not imply funding, partners, dates, promised outputs, recruitment, findings or a commitment by the lab.

## Boundaries and care

Copy must not assume that evidence transfers unchanged, that policy adoption equals delivery, that climate action is automatically beneficial or equitable, or that a quantified ranking can replace academic judgement. Work concerning Indigenous or community knowledge must not unilaterally specify research governance that belongs with appropriate partners.
