Gate 5H prepares the existing Draft 0.1 pull request for owner review by making the public language more direct without changing the lab's academic claims. The concern was valid: Gate 5G was coherent, but four parallel negative propositions, repeated abstract-noun lists, identical research-idea headings and one Work-page template created an institutional cadence.

## What we changed

The homepage now leads with “Cities learn from one another. The hard part is knowing what can travel.” Its supporting copy uses policies, resources, delivery and household costs to explain the research problem. All required sections remain, but the visible main copy is 607 words rather than 722, a 15.9% reduction.

Each theme begins with a labelled practical example before the full existing argument. A controlled map explains generalisability, transportability, appraisal, consequential uncertainty, delivery configuration, co-benefits/co-costs, evidence infrastructure, causal inference and value of information at first use.

Research-idea cards retain all 24 exact questions and complete YAML. Publicly, the problem and consequence now form one paragraph, “One possible approach” replaces repeated scaffolding, no more than three method tags appear, and only signature ideas add a “Who might use the answer” line.

Work pages now vary by type: ongoing programmes/lines, the standalone paper, completed projects and the public tool each have a suitable structure. The panels show five orientation fields; provenance and related themes remain available below, while full taxonomies stay in YAML. Our Approach now presents the hypothetical active-travel example before the six breakdown states. Current Conversations now begins with familiar source types before introducing horizon scanning, source environments and deterministic grouping.

Selected previous-work IDs, order, themes, evidence and qualifications are unchanged. Their contribution statements now use active subjects and varied openings; every change is in the before-and-after audit.

## Challenges and decisions

The hard part was shortening the homepage while adding the owner-specified explanatory hero. We removed repeated summaries, not sections or claims. Another tension was explaining technical language without clutter: definitions appear in one compact inline paragraph before formal prose, with no tooltip dependency. The diagnostic deliberately reports repetition and long sentences but never scores or rewrites prose; canonical titles and necessary qualifications remain even when they are long.
