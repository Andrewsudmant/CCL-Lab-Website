# Gate 5H non-academic reader comprehension audit

This is an editorial comprehension audit, not user testing. It asks whether the final rendered copy gives an interested non-academic reader enough information to answer the required questions.

## Homepage — pass

- **What the lab studies:** stated in the hero through four linked problems: existing evidence, useful new evidence, delivery and consequences.
- **Why learning from another city is difficult:** the supporting paragraph names differences in institutions, infrastructure, resources, delivery and who bears costs.
- **The four questions:** named and described on four numbered cards.
- **Where to go next:** two hero actions, three audience pathways and Work/Current Conversations/contact routes remain visible.

## Theme pages — pass

Each theme begins with a labelled practical scenario, then retains its full argument, proposition and boundary. Ongoing and selected previous work illustrate the distinction. The scenario is not labelled as evidence or a finding. Inline definitions explain relevant specialist terms before the formal argument uses them.

## Work pages — pass

Programmes and research lines explain why work began, what the lab is trying to understand, its approach, present status and what not to infer. The paper centres its question and evidence. Completed projects distinguish outputs from later interpretation. The tool states what it shows, how it can be used and what users must not infer. A concise panel answers type, status, main theme, geography and methods.

## Research ideas — pass

Every card keeps the exact approved question, a combined explanation of why it matters, one possible approach and no more than three method tags. The section-level notice is visible and the full non-active/non-funded status is present for assistive technology on each card. The G5 governance qualification and E1 no-ranking boundary remain visible.

## Current Conversations — pass

The landing page says why the feature is being built, that it is not live and that appearance will not mean endorsement or applicability. The methods page introduces technical language only after describing the process in ordinary terms. It retains source, evidence, security, review and correction safeguards.

## Editorial limitations

Some formal theme paragraphs and canonical publication titles remain long because Gate 5H preserves academic arguments and bibliographic metadata. The diagnostic lists these for judgement rather than treating sentence length as proof of poor quality. Actual reader research would still be valuable after Draft 0.1 is published.
