# Theme and project mapping

## Old-to-new theme mapping

This table records migration logic, not one-to-one conceptual equivalence.

| Former top-level theme | New treatment |
|---|---|
| Urban climate learning and evidence transfer | Primarily **Geographies of Climate Learning**; strategic future-evidence questions may connect to Theme 2. |
| Urban climate evidence infrastructure and decision-support tools | Tools and methods become facets. Records map to **Where New Evidence Matters** only when they concern consequential evidence needs; otherwise they may be cross-cutting or unclassified. |
| Climate governance and delivery modes | **Modes of Climate Delivery**, with the definition narrowed to enabling configurations rather than policy or actor lists. |
| Co-benefits, co-costs and place-based valuation | **Consequences for People and Places**. |
| Just transitions, occupations and workforce change | **Consequences for People and Places**, with delivery and evidence-production links recorded secondarily where supported. |
| Canadian climate policy | Canada and British Columbia become geography/topic facets. Canadian records map to a research theme according to their analytical contribution. |

## Project mapping

| Project | Primary theme | Secondary themes | Rationale and boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Geography of urban climate evidence | Geographies of Climate Learning | Where New Evidence Matters | Existing evidence geography is primary; consequential gaps are a secondary question. Geography alone does not establish transferability. |
| Data Methodologies for Climate Impact Assessment | Where New Evidence Matters | Consequences for People and Places | Scaling choices affect what local consequences can be assessed; no universal scaling method is claimed. |
| Climate delivery modes | Modes of Climate Delivery | Geographies of Climate Learning | Focuses on configurations of authority, finance, coordination, capability and maintenance across places. |
| CoBen | Consequences for People and Places | Where New Evidence Matters; Modes of Climate Delivery | Examines benefits, burdens, costs and distribution while exposing evidence and implementation conditions. Scenarios are conditional, not predictions. |
| Occupational transition requirements | Consequences for People and Places | Where New Evidence Matters; Modes of Climate Delivery | Examines workforce changes and burdens, with evidence-calibration and institutional-delivery connections. It does not predict individual transitions. |
| UK Co-Benefits Atlas | Consequences for People and Places | Where New Evidence Matters; Modes of Climate Delivery | Makes place-based benefits and costs visible. UK estimates are not asserted to transfer universally. |

Every project retains separate geography, climate-domain, sector and method facets. Optional schema fields were populated only from already approved or authoritative project material.
